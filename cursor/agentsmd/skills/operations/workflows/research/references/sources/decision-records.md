# Trackers, documents, and discussion

Discover available connectors and their real query schema. Vendor names are
examples of evidence categories, not required dependencies. Search only the
authorized topic and work context. Reading a discussion never authorizes posting
to its participants.

## Tracker evidence

Begin with linked IDs, then search technical and business descriptions of the
same problem. Read comments and history as well as the current body. Follow
parents, duplicates, incident links, milestones, and project context. A deadline
or customer requirement may explain a compromise absent from the code review.

Check dates and scope changes. Present-day acceptance criteria can postdate the
original decision. Labels, assignees, and resolved status are hints, not a causal
explanation or proof of a fix. Preserve distinction between a report, a proposed
solution, an accepted commitment, and an observed outcome.

## Design and meeting records

Read complete promising documents, linked alternatives, relevant child pages,
and decision comments. Identify author, date, approved versus draft status, and
the revision contemporaneous with the code. The newest document may describe a
later redesign. Compare intended architecture with the artifact actually shipped.

Broaden by concept, symptom, and team vocabulary when links are absent. Keep the
search bounded to relevant work material. A connected notebook containing
unrelated personal material is not a reason to inspect it.

## Discussion evidence

Search exact symbols, errors, PR URLs, feature names, likely channels, authors,
and time windows. Read the whole relevant thread, including the reply that may
reverse the initial claim. Preserve attribution, date, and permalink. Casual
speculation, jokes, and forwarded claims are weaker than an explicit decision
from its owner.

Retention cliffs, inaccessible messages, missing replies, and failed access are
coverage limits. Silence does not prove no deliberation occurred. When an
incident appears, connect its original thread, postmortem, action item, fix, and
release. Separate immediate mitigation from the root cause and longer-term
prevention; a link repeated across tools is not independent corroboration.
