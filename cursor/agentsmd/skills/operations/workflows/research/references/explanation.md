# Teach from evidence

Infer the reader's purpose and prior knowledge from the request. Choose the few
concepts needed for their next decision or action. Introduce each unfamiliar
concept with a plain definition and a concrete example before dependent detail.
Explain a mechanism through what happens to an actual input, event, or user action.

Build understanding in layers: the overall model, a representative path, then
the exceptions that change the conclusion. A reference lists components; an
explanation shows why their relationship matters. Keep claims about historical
purpose tied to [history](history.md), and label inferred rationale.

Use a small diagram when it clarifies ownership, sequence, or state. If a diagram
is too dense, reveal the relationships in stages. Do not manufacture a picture
for every explanation or quiz the reader unless requested. A request for depth
deserves the complete explanation, not forced pauses after a few sentences.

When the user is confused, restate the main claim in familiar language, supply
the missing concept or example, and stop defending the earlier wording. Preserve
uncertainty, negation, and exceptions. Removing caveats to sound confident teaches
the wrong model.

For a durable human guide, use [technical-writing](../../technical-writing/index.md) to choose tutorial, how-to,
reference, or explanation structure. Explaining code remains read-only unless
the user also requested a change.
