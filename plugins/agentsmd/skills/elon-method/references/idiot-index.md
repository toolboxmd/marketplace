# Idiot index

Use this reference when a price, quote, fee, timeline, headcount, or tool cost
is treated as given. Apply it to physical goods, digital products, and services.

Idiot index = quoted cost of the finished thing / cost of its constituents at
commodity prices, for the same outcome, scope, and comparable units.

Constituents include materials, compute, storage, bandwidth, tokens, licenses,
and necessary skilled work. Account for required integration, reliability,
support, proof, compliance, and risk costs when estimating a feasible rebuilt
path. A raw-input floor alone is not a delivery estimate.

## Required answer

1. Quoted: identify the source, scope, number, and unit.
2. Constituents: itemize required inputs and their sourced or estimated costs.
   Distinguish measured values, estimates, and unknowns.
3. Index: divide only comparable quantities. Normalize money to the same
   currency and period. For timelines, compare elapsed durations for the same
   scope and dependency assumptions; labor hours are not elapsed time. For
   headcount, compare equivalent capacity over the same period, skill mix,
   and workload; people are not person-hours. If normalization or input evidence
   is unavailable, report the index as unknown and name the missing evidence.
   Label time or capacity comparisons as analogous ratios, not monetary cost
   indices.
4. Floor if rebuilt: state the supported lower bound, the concrete path toward
   it, and the full necessary costs and uncertainties of that path.

A high index is a diagnostic hypothesis, not proof of waste or its cause.
Investigate packaging, margin, coordination, hidden work, and risk before
choosing an intervention. Constituent cost is a floor, not a promise. If
constituents cannot be named, use first principles to identify them.

Do not invent false precision or waive safety, law, or user authority.
The index is not a deletion quota.
